# Waitlist page (public/waitlist.html)

Scope: the public waitlist and build-log page. Mode: Persuade. Audience: tennis players arriving from Reddit, X and Threads posts. Job: understand what CourtSide is, believe it is real and moving, leave an email for early iPhone access. Structure pinned by the owner: his friend's build-log draft (headline, four features, a five-set progress board, waitlist form, feedback form), rebuilt on CourtSide's own design system. Constraints: no fabricated users, coaches, numbers, testimonials or press; screens must be real captures from the owner's own account; coaching is not live and must read as in progress.

Unresolved: first-person voice ("we") and the contact address (the friend's personal email) await the owner; the four app screens are temporary stand-ins until the owner's iPhone screenshots arrive.

## Direction contract

THESIS: A build log you can believe — the app's own quiet paper-and-club-green world, proven by real screens and an honest score, instead of the category's glossy hero and fake social proof.

OWN-WORLD: CourtSide's palette (#F8F7F2 paper, #3F7049 club green, #24251F court ink, hairline tan borders), platform system type set tight and heavy at display sizes, pill actions, 12px inputs, 16px containers, flat surfaces with shadow only on the phones that float. Dark mode follows the app's dark palette.

STORY: The visitor reads that CourtSide is being built in the open, sees what it does and what's live, sees real screens, reads the score (three sets done, coaching in play, launch next), and leaves an email to get the iPhone app before the App Store.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Left, the headline "CourtSide is getting built, one set at a time." at 72px, the pitch, an inline email field with the green "Get early access" pill, and the promise line. Right, a real feed screen in an iPhone tilted 2 degrees.

FORM: owner-pinned structure (friend's build-log draft); surface roll 0867550c declined in favour of the pin.

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
